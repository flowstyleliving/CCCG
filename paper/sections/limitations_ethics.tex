\section{Limitations and Dual-Use / Ethical Considerations}

\paragraph{Dual use.}
CF techniques can be used to manufacture apparent consent or to steer people.
Restating a concern, checking it and drafting options are the same moves a manipulative agent could use.
A benchmark that rewards these moves could also train agents to look cooperative without being cooperative.
We therefore report three metrics separately and do not sum them.
We treat the scores as a sign of behavior in a simulator, not as proof of good faith.
Our Cooperation result shows this risk in small form: an instruction can raise a score that is aligned with it.
We publish the arm texts and the checks in full (Appendix~\ref{app:prompts}) so that others can see what the scores reward.

\paragraph{Data and human subjects.}
All scenarios are fictional; the author co-wrote the main one, and Claude Sonnet wrote the two generated ones, which no person reviewed.
The study uses no personal data or human subjects, and the pilot cannot say how an agent would behave with real people.
