\section{Discussion}

\subsection{What the metrics show}
Keeping the three metrics separate paid off even in a small pilot.
Cooperation and Candor moved in opposite directions under CF (though the Candor change is uncertain); a summed score would have hidden this.

\paragraph{Cooperation is sensitive, but partly aligned with the pilot instruction.}
The hidden-need checks separated agents most.
Without CF, agents rarely surfaced the sisters' underlying needs.
But these checks reward asking about needs, and the CF text instructs exactly that.
Part of the CF effect may be instruction-following measured by an aligned rubric, not better facilitation in a wider sense.
MSRAF needs Cooperation checks that no arm text names directly.

\paragraph{The Corrigibility checks are too easy.}
Corrigibility was near 1.00 in every cell.
This does not show the agents are corrigible under pressure.
In this scenario nobody strongly corrected the agent, so the checks mostly asked whether it avoided pressure and invited a ``no'', which is easy.
The checks need correction-pressure events: a stakeholder who objects to the agent's own plan, a point person who tries to decide for others, or a mid-episode instruction to stop.
They also need justified-proceeding cases, where the right move is to continue despite an objection, so that blind deference does not score as corrigible.

\paragraph{Candor catches real failures but mixes in a turn-budget effect.}
Many agents never wrote a final report within 12 turns, which lowers \texttt{cand\_finished} in every arm.
The truthfulness checks were not applicable whenever there was no report, so they rest on 25 of 37 episodes.
Proactive flagging of consequential problems, which the Candid definition asks for, is not yet a separate check.

\paragraph{The judge is the weakest link.}
A single LLM judge scored every check, and no person reviewed its verdicts.
Checks such as \texttt{corr\_silence} or \texttt{cand\_relay\_accurate} could instead be computed by rules over the event log.
Until they are, MSRAF scores are the judge's reading of the episode, not deterministic facts about it.

\subsection{What the pilot shows about the gym}
The pilot shows that MSRAF can detect a prompt-level change: the CF toolkit raised Cooperation by 0.29.
The AP framing had no detectable effect.
This may mean the framing does little, or it may reflect a confound: the AP text mentions ``NCEs'' and ``the list'', which only the CF text defines.

\subsection{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
  \item One LLM judge (Nemotron 3 Ultra) scored all episodes, with no human or second-judge check.
  \item The 18 checks were drafted on the day of the run, are not frozen or versioned, and no outside person reviewed them.
  \item Justified-proceeding cases are not implemented, so the rubric cannot yet tell judgment from deference.
  \item Corrigibility is at ceiling; Cooperation checks are aligned with the CF instruction.
  \item We specified three scenarios but ran only one, with at most three seeds per cell; replicates differ only through sampling. Hy4 has one episode.
  \item All stakeholders are played by one model, GPT-6 Luna, whose replies may be more alike than real people's.
  \item The pilot controls are matched by characters, not tokens. The CF toolkit is our condensation, not Kashtan's text, and is not compared with trained facilitators.
  \item Episodes are capped at 12 turns, and fewer than half ended with a final report. The author proposes a minimum of 15 turns for a realistic run (an earlier 5-turn minimum was judged too short, and 3-turn smoke runs too short to use). This pilot is below that minimum, so its results describe a short horizon only. We did not rerun at 15 or more turns because the paid API budget ran out.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Future work}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Move checks that can be computed from the event log into deterministic rules, and check the judge against human raters and a second judge.
  \item Freeze and version the rubric before the next run.
  \item Add correction-pressure events and justified-proceeding cases to the Corrigibility checks.
  \item Add a Candor check for proactively flagging consequential problems.
  \item Add Cooperation checks that no arm text names directly.
  \item Run the two generated scenarios and more seeds, with stakeholder models from more than one developer.
  \item In the pilot, remove CF terms from the AP text and match controls by tokens.
\end{itemize}
