\section{Results}

\paragraph{What changed from the submitted version.}
The submitted version reported 37 episodes (API credit ran out during the Hy4 runs, leaving Hy4 one episode), scored them with rubric v1 and pooled episodes across models, and reported a CF Cooperation effect of $+0.29$ [$+0.20$, $+0.38$].
This version completes the grid (48 episodes), rescores every episode with rubric v2 (Section~\ref{sec:runs} and the judge audit above), and uses the stratified estimator.
Together these lower the CF Cooperation effect to $+0.18$. We also add two generated scenarios and a no-cap run.

Unless noted, results come from the 48 main-grid episodes on the family-will scenario ($n=3$ per cell).
\subsection{What each metric caught}
The per-check counts (Appendix~\ref{app:perchecks}, Table~\ref{tab:perchecks}) show what each metric separates.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Cooperation} discriminated most.
  With CF off versus on, \texttt{coop\_need\_A} passed 5/22 versus 14/24, \texttt{coop\_need\_C} 0/22 versus 14/24 and \texttt{coop\_need\_B} 2/22 versus 9/24.
  \item \textbf{Corrigibility} was at ceiling: five of six checks passed in every episode, and \texttt{corr\_adjusts} passed 22/23 and 24/24.
  \item \textbf{Candor} mostly tracked whether a final report was written.
  \texttt{cand\_finished} passed in 10/24 episodes with CF off and 6/24 with CF on.
  Where a report existed, \texttt{cand\_report\_true} and \texttt{cand\_unresolved} passed in every case (16/16).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Scores by model and arm}
Appendix Table~\ref{tab:scores} and Figure~\ref{fig:scores} give each cell.
Corrigibility arm means were 0.97 (neutral + placebo) and 1.00 elsewhere; Table~\ref{tab:across} gives the Cooperation and Candor arm means.
For every model, both CF arms score higher on Cooperation than the baseline, although GLM 5.3 Flash scored higher in neutral + AP (0.78) than in CF + AP (0.56).

\subsection{Pilot demonstration: CF and AP effects}
Table~\ref{tab:effects} gives the stratified main effects; Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:effects} plots them.
CF raised Cooperation by $+0.18$ [$+0.10$, $+0.26$].
CF changed Candor by $-0.06$ [$-0.12$, $-0.00$], an interval that just reaches zero, so we treat it as weak evidence at most; it follows from fewer final reports in CF arms.
CF changed Corrigibility by $+0.01$ [$+0.00$, $+0.04$], too small to interpret at ceiling.
AP showed no detectable effect: Cooperation $+0.04$ [$-0.05$, $+0.13$], Candor $+0.03$ [$-0.02$, $+0.10$], Corrigibility $+0.01$.
We did not estimate the CF $\times$ AP interaction.

\subsection{Other scenarios and the turn limit}
Table~\ref{tab:across} compares the main grid with the two generated scenarios and the no-cap run.
In ``shared trailer'' the CF Cooperation effect was $+0.19$.
In ``office coffee machine'' it was $0.00$, because the neutral arm was already near ceiling (0.94).
So the CF Cooperation gain appeared in two of three scenarios.
With one seed per cell, these runs give no meaningful interval; only 3 of 12 and 4 of 12 episodes wrote a final report within 15 turns.
In the no-cap run, all four DeepSeek episodes finished on their own, after 40, 28, 31 and 18 turns (neutral + placebo, CF + placebo, neutral + AP, CF + AP), and all wrote a final report, so Candor was 1.00 in each.
With $n=4$ this is descriptive only, but it suggests that part of the Candor deficit in the capped runs came from the cap, not from the agents.
